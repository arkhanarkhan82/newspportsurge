The boxing schedule covers championship bouts, pay-per-view headliners, and undercard fights across heavyweight, middleweight, and every division in between. Sportsurge keeps upcoming boxing cards with dates, times, and Watch links so you never miss a main event.

Upcoming Boxing Fights

Major cards land on weekends throughout the year, with networks and streaming partners rotating by promotion. The table above lists current matchups as they are announced.

  • Heavyweight championship fights – the sport’s marquee division
  • Super middleweight and middleweight – deep talent pools with frequent title defenses
  • Junior welterweight and welterweight – high-volume action cards
  • Women’s titles – championship bouts across multiple weights

See also the MMA schedule for mixed martial arts cards on the same weekends, and the full sports schedule for every league.

How Boxing Cards Are Structured

A fight card builds from early preliminaries to a televised undercard and the main event. Championship fights are typically 12 rounds; most non-title bouts are 8 to 10 rounds. Broadcast partners in the US include DAZN, ESPN, Prime Video, and traditional PPV for select headliners.

How to Watch Boxing

Availability depends on the card. Some events stream on subscription services like DAZN or ESPN+; others are standalone PPV. Check the listed time and Watch link for each fight before the night of the card.

Major Boxing Promotions and Networks

Different promoters control different stages of the sport. Matchroom (DAZN), Top Rank (ESPN), and PBC cards (various networks and PPV) book most of the high-profile title fights. Heavyweight championship bouts and undisputed super-middleweight matchups tend to draw the biggest audiences and the longest undercards.

When a card is announced, the schedule above updates with the date, ring walks estimate, and broadcast details so you can plan the night around the main event.
